# Organizing knowledge to change the questions artificial researchers discover

## Research question and contribution

Artificial researchers can search thousands of papers and produce plausible proposals, yet access to more text does not ensure that they understand what a field means by its concepts. Two studies may use the same label for different phenomena; two communities may use different labels for similar ideas. Measures can overlap while definitions diverge. An agent that compresses these distinctions into a single summary can generate fluent questions that merely restate existing work or join variables without an explanatory puzzle. We ask: **How does an organized, conceptually accountable representation of fragmented organizational knowledge change the theoretical questions that artificial researchers discover?**

The scientific object is the organization of knowledge available to the researcher, not the volume of generated text. We will test whether exposing agents to traceable construct meanings, measures, and unresolved conceptual relations changes the novelty, theoretical importance, and nonredundancy of the questions they produce when the underlying papers are held fixed. A second comparison tests whether specialists working across community boundaries find questions that one generalist or isolated specialist misses. Our aim is to learn when AI helps researchers explore a fragmented intellectual landscape, and when apparently new ideas reflect poor retrieval or conceptual confusion.

The pilot connects Communication, Digital Technology, and Organization (CTO) with Technology and Innovation Management (TIM). Both study digital change, but often at different levels and with different accounts of work, organizing, and innovation. Their overlap creates opportunities for theoretical translation without treating either community as a single topic. Conceptual *jingle* (one label, different meanings) and *jangle* (different labels, similar meanings) are particularly useful stress tests. They are candidate relationships to investigate, not equivalences that an embedding score can declare true (Casper et al., 2018; Carlile, 2004).

## Research artifact

The artifact has two linked components. A pipeline begins with a versioned bibliographic catalog, canonical paper identities, community memberships, and source provenance. For accessible texts, it extracts construct definitions and meaning profiles separately from measures, units of analysis, and claims. Semantic, lexical, and citation-based signals retrieve possible conceptual relationships. Each candidate dossier keeps the source passages, competing explanations, missing information, and the status of any adjudication. Accepted and unresolved relationships remain distinct. A release records the corpus perimeter, extraction and retrieval versions, quality checks, and whether it has actually passed a validation gate.

A discovery forum then gives agents controlled views of the same literature. In condition **0**, an agent searches source passages without the organized construct layer. In **A**, a generalist receives the passages and structured records. In **B**, one CTO or TIM specialist works alone on its community view; each repetition includes both specialist variants. In **C**, the specialists hold an open encounter around an evidence-based theme. Agents may begin from a phenomenon, anomaly, challenged assumption, paradox, missing mechanism, boundary reversal, process, theoretical translation, or construct problem. Jingle and jangle are subtypes of the construct-problem trigger, not separate experimental arms. The forum records the question, proposed mechanism, discriminating prediction, retrieval trace, citations, prompts, model settings, and actual resource use.

The code already implements the pipeline and the four-condition forum. Its deeper extraction and release gates have been exercised end to end on a synthetic corpus, **not validated on the full real corpus**. Real forum episodes can run against a provisional release and are marked accordingly. The current evaluator searches a 2015–2025 CTO/TIM abstract subset of 10,050 papers; that is a bounded search collection, not proof of complete prior-art coverage or a validated knowledge base. The repository also contains a self-contained reviewer edition: an invented four-paper case shows how the same passages yield a possible cross-level question once construct profiles are visible. It computes candidate jingle/jangle relations and checks quotations. The two displayed questions are explicitly scripted illustrations, not measured agent outcomes. This small artifact lets a reviewer inspect the proposed mechanism without a database account, licensed abstracts, or model API key.

## Discovery experiment

The main comparison is **0 versus A**: does access to organized conceptual evidence change discovery beyond access to the same underlying papers? **A versus B** probes specialization; **B versus C** probes cross-community dialogue. We will freeze the corpus, release, model, prompt family, trigger distribution, document entry points, retrieval allowance, and per-episode time and call limits before comparing conditions. Actual tokens and papers seen will be recorded, because equal ceilings do not guarantee equal exposure. CTO and TIM openers and triggers will be counterbalanced. B yields two separate specialist outputs per repetition, so we will report both quality per question and the number of distinct, high-quality questions per fixed resource budget. Within-community and cross-community tasks will be analyzed separately; an isolated specialist should not be penalized for evidence it could not access.

Primary outcomes are independent judgments of novelty after prior-art checking, theoretical importance, explanatory clarity, and the presence of a discriminating mechanism and prediction. We will also measure conceptual diversity through coded idea families, rather than only distance between generated sentences. Our causal claim concerns the effect of representation and agent organization under the stated experimental conditions, not a universal ranking of architectures. Expert assessment of substantive quality and construct relationships is part of the planned validation phase. The present prototype runs without continuous human attention, but its automated scores remain exploratory until that assessment exists.

Before the main comparison, we will select a fixed set of open starting problems and record which papers support each one. Episodes will be assigned to conditions within those problem blocks, with run order randomized and evaluators blind to condition. The unit of analysis is the episode; multiple questions from an episode will remain clustered. We will publish the full distribution of outcomes, abstentions, retrieval failures, and resource use rather than report only the highest scoring ideas. A question will count as distinct only if its explanatory target, mechanism, or prediction differs in a way a reader can articulate. This design also lets us ask when a construct problem helps: the map may matter most when a shared label conceals different levels of analysis, while other triggers may benefit little. That interaction is a hypothesis, not a conclusion drawn from the current pilot.

## Evaluating the evaluator

The same system that generates a question can be fooled when judging it. We therefore treat evaluation as an object of study. A versioned novelty search will examine the declared eligible catalog with both semantic and lexical retrieval, deduplicate by canonical paper identity, and show what years and texts were unavailable. A judge compares the proposed **question**, **mechanism**, and **discriminating predictions** separately with exact proposal and paper passages. Similar vocabulary does not by itself imply duplication, and an unquoted assertion of difference does not establish novelty. When the corpus boundary, missing proposal detail, or contradictory evidence prevents a defensible decision, the evaluator must return **insufficient evidence** with a reason rather than manufacture a score.

Methodology assessment asks whether the proposed design, measures, units, and identification strategy could test the claimed mechanism against alternatives. It marks design choices inferred by an agent rather than supplied by the proposal. Impact assessment keeps the possible contribution of a question separate from citations and journal indicators describing prior papers. We will test retrieval with known-answer and paraphrase controls, then vary evidence packets under a frozen judge and matched extra-paper controls to identify sensitivity to retrieval. Independent experts will later test whether these traces and ratings correspond to scientific quality. A blinded evaluation of 2026 papers across journal tiers is a supplementary stress test, not an article-level truth label or a substitute for expert assessment.

## Preliminary evidence and limits

The forum has completed **30 real episodes** in two batches, each with three repetitions of the five operational arms (0, A, B-CTO, B-TIM, C). This demonstrates that the controlled workflow can run. It does not show that organization improves questions: the generalist's old blind-text score exceeded the baseline in one batch and fell below it in the other. Among six matched repetitions, A scored higher twice, lower twice, and tied twice. The batches use different triggers and only three repetitions per cell. Their scores cannot identify a treatment effect.

A newer export contains **38 generated proposals** evaluated for novelty, methodology, and impact. An offline audit matched **886 cited proposal/paper passage pairs** to stored text and found **three failed hybrid novelty quote checks**. Among 35 completed hybrid checks, three changed the novelty vote by at least two points. These observations establish that the pipeline can expose unsupported or unstable judgments. Exact quotation is a necessary traceability check, not proof that a comparison is substantively right. A controlled retrieval ablation has been prepared but has not yet produced judge results. We will preserve failures and abstentions in all reported denominators.

An in-corpus lexical retrieval smoke test used 1,000 deterministic source-paper snippets from the 10,050-paper index. The source appeared in the top 20 for all 1,000 unaltered 20-word snippets, 979 after half the rare terms were deleted, and 323 after three quarters were deleted. This tests recovery of known indexed sources under mechanical word removal, not paraphrase handling or novelty accuracy.

The central risks are selection bias in the corpus, incomplete or copyrighted full texts, mistaken construct equivalence, model instability, and evaluators rewarding polished language over theoretical contribution. We address these through versioned boundaries, provisional labels, source-linked dossiers, frozen comparisons, blinded assessment, and explicit failure states. Reviewers can reproduce the public synthetic mechanism case and inspect aggregate pilot counts; licensed text and unpublished proposals require controlled access. The technical supplement identifies exactly which analyses can be recreated from the public artifact and which need the private export. No current release is described as human validated.

If successful, the project will show whether knowledge organization changes what artificial researchers are able to ask, and provide a reusable way to inspect how they reached a question. A negative result would also matter: it would locate where richer representations or specialist dialogue fail to improve discovery, and where apparently persuasive automated novelty judgments collapse under evidence checks. This moves beyond producing more candidate papers toward testing the conditions under which AI can support better organizational science (Alvesson & Sandberg, 2011; Gartenberg et al., 2026).
