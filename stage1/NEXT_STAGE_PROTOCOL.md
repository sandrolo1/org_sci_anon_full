# Next-stage protocol: matched 0/A discovery experiment

**Status, 23 October 2026:** This is an executable plan for a future study, not a preregistration or a claim that condition A improves discovery. It is separate from the anonymous Stage 1 upload. The [Organization Science call](https://orgsci.org/) selects Stage 1 projects on novelty, feasibility, auditability, ethicality, reproducibility, scalability, adoption, and potential to expand the research frontier. Selected teams develop the work in Stage 2 (January–May 2027), including an expected in-person workshop; the [editors' FAQ](https://orgsci.substack.com/p/the-portal-is-open) says reviewers should be able to verify the artifact and that anonymity extends to repository contents. The official call permits a Git repository **or equivalent artifact**, while the FAQ describes a GitHub submission; check the portal and use a fresh anonymous repository built from the reviewed artifact if a URL is required. Neither source asks for a positive effect at Stage 1.

## What the pilot establishes

The saved real export contains 30 completed episodes: two batches, each with three repetitions of conditions 0, A, B-CTO, B-TIM, and C. On the older `proposal-blind-v2` rubric, A beat 0 in two of their six matched repetitions, trailed in two, and tied in two. Its mean was about 0.33 higher in one batch and 0.34 lower in the other. The batches differed in trigger and resource configuration, and the current batch code runs arms in fixed order. These observations establish operational feasibility only. They do **not** estimate the effect below or calibrate a power calculation. The 2026 paper-tier exercise and quote-matching audit are separate evaluator checks, not validation of generated question quality.

## Causal contrast and frozen inputs

**Question:** Holding the research problem and resources fixed, does access to a source-linked conceptual map improve the quality of an AI-generated research question relative to access to the same underlying literature without that map?

The assignment unit is an episode, paired by a frozen problem packet and seed. Condition **0** receives the packet's eligible source passages. Condition **A** receives those *same* passages plus a versioned map of construct meanings, measures, candidate overlaps, and provenance. Both use the same model release, decoding settings, common prompt skeleton, retrieval interface, clock and token ceilings, and evaluation rules. The map's candidate relations remain labeled provisional unless independently adjudicated. This isolates the *availability* of the map; trace logs separately measure whether A actually uses it. If passage exposure cannot be made equivalent, report the contrast as a broader interface intervention rather than attributing it to conceptual organization alone.

Before any confirmatory run, freeze and hash: (1) the eligible paper perimeter and canonical-ID mapping, licensing/shareability list, source snapshots, and map release; (2) a registry of problem packets spanning CTO, TIM, and cross-community questions, with each packet's trigger, seed, passage IDs, and content hash; (3) prompts, model identifiers, tool access, output schema, evaluation rubric, exclusion/failure rules, and analysis script; and (4) a common per-episode resource cap. Keep external web access off during the contrast. Do not select packets after seeing arm outcomes. The current five-arm batch runner uses fixed 0-then-A order; a 0/A-only runner must consume the randomized schedule before the study can be called randomized.

`python3 stage1/plan_matched_0a.py --blocks frozen_blocks.json --assignment-seed <recorded-random-seed> --out schedule.json` creates a deterministic, balanced within-stratum schedule and refuses to overwrite an existing schedule. The input contains opaque IDs and hashes, not licensed text. Freeze the block registry first, draw and record the assignment seed independently of outcomes, then archive both files and their hashes. The scheduler does not run models or prove passage equivalence; the runner must verify the latter for every pair.

## Outcomes, blinding, and analysis

Each episode submits one designated final question with its mechanism and at least one prediction. The **primary outcome** is mean independently rated question quality on a locked 0–10 codebook, comparing A minus 0 within pairs. The codebook must define anchors for theoretical importance, mechanism clarity, and the prediction's ability to distinguish rival explanations. A run that produces no designated question receives 0 and remains in the intention-to-treat denominator. Two independent domain assessors rate each output; each assessor sees at most one arm of a pair, and assignment of assessors to arms is balanced. The assessment view removes condition and map labels, run order, agent traces, and model metadata without rewriting scientific content. Record assessors' treatment guesses to measure residual unblinding; retain their original scores regardless of guesses.

An assessor may mark an output **unrateable** with a reason instead of inventing a score. Report this rate by arm. Do not silently drop those pairs: show complete-case paired differences and conservative 0/10 bounds for unresolved scores; make no confirmatory superiority claim if plausible missing-score assignments reverse it. Automated novelty review has a separate `insufficient_evidence` state. It must search the full *frozen eligible* catalog with semantic and lexical retrieval, deduplicate canonical IDs, cite passages distinguishing question, mechanism, and predictions, and abstain where coverage is inadequate. Methodology asks whether the design can test the mechanism; prospective importance and existing-paper citations are distinct impact outputs. These are secondary diagnostics, not substitutes for blind question-quality ratings.

Estimate the paired A−0 mean and 95% confidence interval under the predeclared analysis. If several seeds share a problem, use problem-clustered uncertainty (for example, a cluster bootstrap with the problem as the resampling unit); do not treat outputs or assessors as independent experimental units. One primary two-sided test uses α = 0.05, with all secondary outcomes reported as exploratory estimates and intervals. Publish the full distribution, denominator, failures, unrateability, rater disagreement, and any deviations. Do not stop for significance. A negative or uncertain result is a valid boundary finding under the [call](https://orgsci.org/).

## Feasibility gate and sample-size sensitivity

Run **12 pairs as a separate feasibility phase**, provisionally four in each of the CTO, TIM, and cross-community strata. Use it to measure passage-equivalence failures, output failures, rater agreement, paired-score variability, and tokens/calls/wall time. Finalize the map release, rubric, smallest meaningful difference, sample size, and analysis before any confirmatory outputs are generated. Exclude these 12 pairs from the confirmatory estimate. This planned expert-rating phase is later research, not a dependency of the current automated prototype. If the map is not source-auditable or the blind rating process fails, repair and repeat feasibility rather than advancing.

For planning only, a paired normal approximation at two-sided α = 0.05 and 80% power gives `ceil[(2.80 × SD(pair difference) / meaningful difference)^2]` complete pairs. These scenarios are **not** power estimates from the six pilot pairs:

| Assumed paired SD | Difference 0.5 points | Difference 0.75 points |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.75 | 18 pairs | 8 pairs |
| 1.00 | 32 pairs | 14 pairs |
| 1.50 | 71 pairs | 32 pairs |

With a provisional 10% allowance for unresolved ratings, 32 and 71 complete pairs imply recruiting about 36 and 79 pairs. The table assumes independent pairs; multiple seeds from one problem reduce effective information, so account for problem clustering when fixing the actual target. Use the feasibility phase's *variance and failure rates*, a scientific minimum difference, and the available budget; do not choose the target from the observed A−0 mean. If that target is unaffordable, declare a precision-focused or exploratory study and give its confidence interval rather than implying adequate power.

The private pilot's six 0/A pairs used a median of **631,777 generation tokens per pair**, summing recorded forum-message input and output tokens. Thus 12, 36, and 79 pairs imply roughly 7.6, 23, and 50 million generation tokens, respectively, **before** evaluation calls; these are coarse planning envelopes, not cost quotes or caps. Log actual input/output tokens, model calls, retrieval calls, unique papers/passages seen, wall time, failures, and applicable prices for every attempt. Preserve unsuccessful and over-budget attempts. Save raw responses and hashes because replaying a model seed may not reproduce identical text. Share public or license-cleared evidence plus synthetic substitutes where needed; the [call's reproducibility FAQ](https://orgsci.org/) makes sufficient reproducible data a condition of fit for the issue.
